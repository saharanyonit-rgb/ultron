## 1. What is Reported Speech?

Reported speech is used to **repeat what someone has already said** — you take a message you heard from one place and pass it on to someone else.

| | Direct Speech | Reported (Indirect) Speech |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | The speaker says it **first-hand, in person** | You **relay** it afterwards to another person |
| Signal words | says, tells, asks | said, told, asked |
| Tense | whatever is natural at the time of speaking | mostly **past** |

### Opening example (the "gossip" hook)

> **Aunty said, "When will they have kids?"** — *direct speech* (aunty says it to you)
> **Aunty said when would they have kids.** — *reported speech*

- `said` → `said` (nothing to change)
- `will` → `would`
- question word order stays the same because `when` already carries the inversion

## 2. The Six Rules

### Rule 1 — Reported speech is normally written in the **past tense**

> He said, "I am tired." → **He said that he was tired.**
> She asked, "Where do you live?" → **She asked where I lived.**
> Aunty said, "When will they have kids?" → **Aunty said when would they have kids.**

**Logic:** someone told you something *after* it happened, so it must be past.

#### The only 2 exceptions where tense is NOT changed

| Exception | Direct Speech | Reported Speech | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Universal truth** | The Earth revolves around the Sun. | He said that the Earth revolves around the Sun. | A truth is true in all tenses |
| **Future reference** | I will go to the store tomorrow. | She said that she would go to the store tomorrow. | The future is still the future at reporting time |

Apart from these two cases, everything shifts to past.

---

### Rule 2 — The **myth**: "Reported speech is always third person" — **FALSE**

Reported speech can be written in **first, second or third person**, depending on *who* is speaking.

| Person | Used when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1st person** | The speaker talks about **himself / his own actions or habits** | "I love pizza." → **I said that I loved pizza.** |
| **2nd person** | The speaker reports **what someone said to him** | A teacher told me, "You are a great student." → **The teacher told me that I was a great student.** |
| **3rd person** | The speaker is **someone else** talking about a third person (the most common case) | He said, "I am working late tonight." → **He said that he was working late tonight.** |

> **Exam tip:** most reported-speech questions are *"who said what to whom"*, so 3rd person is the default.

---

### Rule 3 — Commands & requests → **to** / **not to**

| Type | Direct Speech | Reported Speech |
|---|---|---|
| Positive command | He said, "Help me." | She told me **to help** him. |
| Negative command | She said, "Don't forget your bag." | She told me **not to forget** my bag. |

**Positive → `to` · Negative → `not to`**

---

### Rule 4 — Yes/No questions → **`if` or `whether`**

Any statement answerable with *"yes"* or *"no"* gets `if` / `whether` in reported speech. You may use either.

> "Are you going to the party?" → **He asked if/whether I was going to the party.**

This is also used for statements that act as embedded yes/no questions:
> He said, "You are going to the party." → **He asked if I was going to the party.**

---

### Rule 5 — WH-questions → **`asked`** + keep the WH word

WH words = what, where, when, who, why, how, which…

> "Where are the crackers?" → **She asked me where the crackers were.**
> "What is your password?" → **He asked me what my password was.**

**No `if`/`whether` here.** The WH word replaces the auxiliary.

---

### Rule 6 — **Time and place words** shift back

| Direct | Reported | Direct | Reported |
|---|---|---|---|
| today | that day | now | then |
| tomorrow | the next day | at the moment | at that moment |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day | last week | the week before / the previous week |
| tonight | that night | next week | the next week / the following week |

## 3. `said` vs `told` vs `asked` — the most confused part

| Word | Use it when | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **`said`** | General statements; no specific listener named | He said that he was tired. |
| **`told`** | A **specific person** is being addressed (`told + object`) | She **told me** not to forget my bag. |
| **`asked`** | **Only** questions and requests | She asked where I lived. / She asked me to let her out. |

> `told` **always** needs a person after it: *She told **me/him/her/Ram** …*
> Everywhere else → `said`. Requests and questions → `asked`.

## 4. Solved Practice Set (from the video)

| # | Question given | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | She said, "I have a question." | She said that she **had** a question. |
| 2 | She said, "Where are the crackers?" | She asked me where the crackers **were**. |
| 3 | She said, "Let me out." | She told me to let her out. |
| 4 | She said, "Can you speak French?" | She asked if/whether I **could** speak French. |
| 5 | She said, "You are so pretty." | She told me I **was** so pretty. |
| 6 | She said, "What is your password?" | She asked me what my password **was**. |

## 5. Exam-Day Cheat Sheet

1. Change the tense to **past** — except universal truths and future statements.
2. `said` / `asked` in general; `told + person` when someone specific is addressed.
3. **Yes/No** → `asked if / whether …`
4. **WH-question** → `asked + WH word` (no if/whether)
5. **Positive command** → `to + verb` · **Negative command** → `not to + verb`
6. Shift **today / tomorrow / yesterday / now** accordingly.
7. Pick the **person** (1st / 2nd / 3rd) from who is speaking.
